What the audit found

Three things were found by reading rather than running. All three change what earlier results mean, and two of them were errors in my own instruments.

The headline: half of the sliding window's measured harm was an implementation defect, not a finding. And the project's answer to “why doesn't a better teacher help?” was stated too broadly — the two arms that tested it were both structurally incapable of doing so.

01 — confirmed, 5/5 seedsThe window was fed zeroed actions

You rejected my claim that the sliding-window question was “already tested — h27,” and asked for the implementation to be audited against the DT paper. The audit found the history slots existed but carried no action tokens. h196 fixed it.

Paired −2.61 (se 0.93), better on 5/5 seeds. 52% of the window's deficit was the defect. The window still hurts when fed correctly, so the direction of the old null survives — but its magnitude was half artifact, and no claim resting on the window's size is safe to quote.

Neither the identity gate nor code review would have caught this. It took reading Algorithm 1 against our inference path.

02 — my errorI measured b over the wrong set

The real-query information-gain label is fitted from a Gumbel scale b. I computed it over a uniform 200-point pool; training computes it over the ROI-filtered 600-point pool. Size alone breaks it — the estimator depends on |X| in both location and scale.

pool usednet change in bsteps down / up
uniform, 200 pts+2.7%13 / 12
ROI-filtered, 600 pts−27.9%14 / 9

I was one step from concluding “the information signal isn't there at inference.” That would have been an artifact of my own pool choice. The rule is now shared by both paths.

03 — retractionThe answer was stated too broadly

The project's answer ended: “a perfect teacher's first move is a random start point, whose average is the middle of the box.” Withdrawn. That is true of the h145 oracle by construction — its path interpolates from a Uniform(domain) start, so its first action is literally noise — but it is not a property of good teachers.

Your framing: an expert teacher makes the optimal decision at every step, not one that merely arrives somewhere good.

This makes the mechanism sharper, not weaker. If the model emits its teacher's first-step mean, then teacher quality helps iff it improves that mean. Re-reading the failures:

h145 — the “oracle”

Uniform random start → its first-step mean is the box centre. It destroyed exactly the signal the model consumes. Its 10/10 failure is evidence about that construction, not about teacher quality.

h152 — the joint-information-gain beam

Optimises argmin b_T — the exact optimum of the reward we wrote down. Its elite node's first child is the greedy move, so its first step barely differs from the control. It could not have moved the outcome either.

Neither arm ever varied the quantity the mechanism says is decisive. That is a gap, not a null.

04 — runningA teacher that optimises the task

h198 is the first teacher here that optimises the task rather than an information criterion or the labelled reward. Each step, it scores candidates by expected terminal best value: condition on a fantasy outcome, let a greedy policy play out the remaining steps, average over fantasies, take the argmax.

It is GP-only — no oracle access, so unlike h145 it is a deployable method rather than a diagnostic ceiling. Both label variants run as a factorial (your “build both and run both”), differing by a single flag.

Gate passed before launch: it reduces exactly to greedy at n_c=1 (6/6), and its first-step action differs from greedy on 8/12 starts — the decisive quantity does move, so a null here would refute sufficiency, not necessity.
