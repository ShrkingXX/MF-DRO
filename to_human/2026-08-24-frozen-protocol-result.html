The near-zero mean difference is not typical-case parity. It is driven almost entirely by seed 45, where MI-Greedy failed badly (1.5396) and MF-DRO did not (0.4331). On the median seed MF-DRO is slightly worse (+0.09). Reporting means alone would have misrepresented this.

The real difference is variance

MI-Greedy has the better ceiling (0.188 vs 0.313); MF-DRO has a far better floor (0.725 vs 1.540) and is 3.2× more consistent.

An honest caveat about the test itself. Its bar is best-baseline mean−SE — set by the baseline’s own instability. MI-Greedy’s large SE drags that bar down to 0.3825, structurally penalising a lower-variance competitor. The test stays frozen and is reported as specified; this is an observation about what it measures, not grounds to change it. But “failed while being 3.2× more consistent” is a different claim from “failed”.

What was actually established

  • A target-leakage bug in the DT readout (both MF and SF paths) invalidated the entire 1280-run prior corpus. Ablation-verified: zeroing the action embedding cost the fidelity head 15.6% of its loss pre-fix vs 0.7% post-fix.
  • Two conflated freeze mechanisms separated. MF-GP-UCB queries HF exactly zero times on all 10 seeds — its freeze is definitional, not the DRO pathology.
  • Two conditioning hypotheses refuted (H4 AdaLN, H5 score-head), both pre-registered, converging on: MF-DRO re-fits rather than conditions.
  • The inherited baseline was never cost-matched. MI-Greedy is 0.509 at matched cost, not the 0.279 this project had been calibrating against.
