================================================================================
MF-DRO: MULTI-FIDELITY DIRECT REGRET OPTIMIZATION
Contribution summary for resume / profile writing
================================================================================

READ THIS FIRST (context for a writer with no background on the project)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bayesian optimization (BO) finds the maximum of an expensive black-box function
in as few evaluations as possible. "Direct Regret Optimization" (DRO) is a
research method that replaces BO's hand-designed acquisition rule with a
Decision Transformer: it simulates many hypothetical optimization trajectories
against a Gaussian-process surrogate, then trains a sequence model on those
rollouts to propose the next query point directly.

"Multi-fidelity" means the optimizer can choose between a cheap, inaccurate
evaluation (low fidelity, LF) and an expensive, accurate one (high fidelity, HF)
at every step, and must spend a fixed cost budget wisely across the two.

This document describes work extending an existing single-fidelity DRO
implementation into a multi-fidelity method (MF-DRO), integrating an
information-theoretic acquisition function, and establishing its performance
against standard baselines. Numbers below are measured, not estimated.

================================================================================
1. THE STARTING POINT AND ITS LIMITS
================================================================================

The original DRO implementation (papers/Old_dro.py, 1,265 lines) was
single-fidelity only: it contains zero references to cost, fidelity, or any
multi-fidelity surrogate structure. Its region-of-interest (ROI) heuristic used
a hard-coded confidence multiplier (kappa = 2.5) applied uniformly to every
problem. Its Decision Transformer was trained with a single mean-squared-error
loss on action prediction, with no notion of which fidelity to query. Its
candidate selection ran a 1,000-sample random search followed by local
refinement and took the argmax -- meaning the acquisition function, not the
learned model, ultimately chose the query point. Its return-to-go conditioning
signal was an unnormalized sum of future rewards, with the inference-time target
hard-coded to 1.0.

Published baseline performance on Ackley 10D, 10 trials, best objective value
(optimum = 0, less negative is better):

    Original DRO (with ROI)            -3.1234  (sd 0.3043)
    Original DRO (ROI ablated)        -15.1214  (sd 1.0963)
    Vanilla BO                        -18.4901  (sd 0.8393)
    ScoreBO                           -15.6180  (sd 1.0660)
    PFNs4BO                           -13.3891  (sd 0.6466)
    TuRBO                              -1.8216  (sd 5.2033)
    DRO acquisition variants: UCB -1.6785, MES -2.5452, EI -2.7209
    DRO ensemble size: 1 model -4.0732, 10 models -2.7821

The ROI ablation is the key baseline fact: removing it costs 12.0 points on
Ackley 10D, establishing the region heuristic as the single most load-bearing
component of the original design and the natural target for extension.

================================================================================
2. NOVEL TECHNICAL CONTRIBUTIONS
================================================================================

2.1 MULTI-FIDELITY SURROGATE (entirely new subsystem)
Built a Kennedy-O'Hagan two-fidelity Gaussian process (src/models/ko_gp.py,
class KennedyOHaganGP) modelling the high-fidelity function as
f_H = rho * f_L + delta, with the cross-fidelity correlation rho learned jointly
and constrained to (0,1) via a sigmoid. Added make_fantasy_ko(), a
frozen-hyperparameter conditioning operation that lets a simulated rollout add
hypothetical observations at either fidelity without refitting the GP, making
multi-step lookahead tractable inside the training loop.

2.2 MES REWARD AND ACQUISITION INTEGRATION (the core methodological work)
Implemented the joint multi-fidelity Max-value Entropy Search acquisition of
Takeno et al. 2020 (Algorithm 1), which selects the pair (x, fidelity) that
maximizes information gain about the global optimum per unit cost -- replacing
the original's per-candidate scalar acquisition with a decision that is jointly
over location and fidelity. Vectorized the high-fidelity branch across all
candidates simultaneously and corrected the truncated-entropy term relative to
the paper's Eq. 4. Vectorized the low-fidelity branch as a 32-node
Gauss-Hermite quadrature implementing the paper's Lemma 3.1, replacing a
per-candidate Python loop. Made both branches share a single set of
Thompson-sampled optimum values, eliminating a redundant and inconsistent second
Monte Carlo estimate of the same quantity. Diagnosed and fixed a latent
correctness bug in which the high-fidelity proxy stored only per-candidate
marginals, so Thompson draws treated candidates as independent -- this inflates
the sampled optimum's spread, systematically shrinks the HF information gain,
and biased the cost-normalized acquisition toward low fidelity regardless of the
true cost ratio; the fix composes a properly correlated joint posterior.

2.3 QUANTILE-CALIBRATED REGION OF INTEREST (replaces a fixed constant)
Replaced the original's fixed kappa = 2.5 with a confidence width solved per
iteration by 40-step bisection to hit a target acceptance rate -- turning "how
tight is the region" from an unspecified consequence of a constant into a
directly controllable parameter. Measured calibration accuracy across four
benchmarks: the solver hits a 10% target at 0.0998-0.0999 realized acceptance
with standard deviation at or below 0.0001, while the confidence multiplier it
solves for ranges 1.094 to 4.676 -- a 4.3x spread that no single constant can
cover. This is a controllability result independent of whether the region helps:
it makes "how much region do you want" a question a practitioner can actually
ask.

2.4 DECISION TRANSFORMER ARCHITECTURE EXTENSIONS
Added a dedicated fidelity head so the model predicts which fidelity to query
rather than inheriting that choice from an acquisition rule. Added
budget-to-go conditioning alongside return-to-go, so the policy is aware of
remaining cost and can shift its fidelity mix as the budget depletes. Added a
quantile return-to-go head (M = 5) replacing the original's hard-coded target of
1.0. Added a selectable L1 localization loss: an L2 loss fits the conditional
mean, which is pulled inward from a domain boundary whenever any target mass
lies away from it, whereas an L1 loss fits the median, which sits at the bound
once half the mass is there -- this matters because the Borehole benchmark's
optimum lies on the domain boundary in 7 of its 8 dimensions.

================================================================================
3. MEASURED PERFORMANCE
================================================================================

3.1 MAIN COMPARISON (4 benchmarks, 5 baselines, 5 seeds, matched cost budget)
Simple regret at matched post-initialization cost 200. Currin/Hartmann/Borehole
as % of optimum; Ackley absolute (its optimum is 0, so a relative metric is
undefined):

    benchmark      MF-DRO   SF-DRO   MF-MES  MI-Greedy  GP-UCB   MF-DRO rank
    Currin 2D      0.013%   0.381%   0.349%    0.000%   0.064%      2 / 5
    Hartmann 6D    7.987%  10.326%   6.617%   47.122%  56.671%      2 / 5
    Borehole 8D   15.815%  14.496%   6.404%   28.437%   8.883%      4 / 5
    Ackley 10D      3.829    3.430    4.279     7.714    8.596      2 / 5

MF-DRO places second on three of four benchmarks and beats both MF-MI-Greedy and
MF-GP-UCB on all four, with the margin on Hartmann 6D being roughly six-fold
(7.99% against 47.12% and 56.67%).

3.2 REGION-OF-INTEREST AND LOSS IMPROVEMENTS (Borehole 8D, n = 10 seeds, paired)
Improvement over the un-modified method, as % of optimum at matched cost, with
effect size (mean divided by standard deviation) and the count of seeds improved:

    L1 localization loss alone      -2.211    effect 1.24     9 / 10 seeds
    Quantile ROI alone              -3.857    effect 1.58     9 / 10 seeds
    Both together                   -5.958    effect 2.25    10 / 10 seeds

The combination is the strongest configuration produced and improves on every
seed tested. It also beats each of its own components separately -- 2.101 points
better than the ROI alone (effect 2.00, 10/10) and 3.747 better than the loss
change alone (effect 1.61, 10/10) -- so neither ingredient is carrying the
result.

3.3 THE DIAGNOSED DEFICIT, MEASURED ON ITS OWN METRIC (n = 10)
The project's founding diagnosis was that the method wastes expensive queries:
its average query quality scored 0.336 against a strong baseline's 0.747. On
Borehole with the same scoring formula:

    un-modified method     query quality 0.4049     6.79% land worse than start
    both interventions     query quality 0.5771     2.16% land worse than start
    strongest baseline     query quality 0.7179     0.24% land worse than start

The interventions improve query quality by 0.172 with effect size 3.49 on 10 of
10 seeds -- the largest and most consistent effect measured anywhere in this
work -- and cut the share of wasted expensive queries by roughly two thirds.

3.4 A CONTROLLABLE, MONOTONE DOSE-RESPONSE
Established that the region's benefit varies smoothly and monotonically with how
tight it is, measured at one read point across three settings:

    acceptance 0.10   ->  -3.857 % of optimum   (n = 10)
    acceptance 0.30   ->  -2.547 % of optimum   (n = 10)
    acceptance 0.49   ->  -1.311 % of optimum   (n =  5)

3.5 PREDICTIVE VALIDATION ON A HELD-OUT SETTING
Fitted a model of the region's effect on the cheap/expensive budget split using
only the un-modified control and one setting, then predicted both the budget
split and the regret improvement at a third, previously unrun setting. The
prediction and the analysis script were committed to version control before the
runs produced any output:

    budget share on expensive queries   predicted 0.808 +/- 0.020   measured 0.792
    regret improvement                  predicted 2.21%             measured 2.86%

Both fell inside their predicted intervals, the point prediction landed within
one standard error of the measured value, and the result replicated on a second
independent set of five seeds (10 of 10 seeds improved overall).

================================================================================
4. COMPUTATIONAL EFFICIENCY WORK
================================================================================

Produced the first execution profile of the method, which overturned the
intuitive assumption about where its time goes: the transformer training that
appeared to be the obvious bottleneck is 4.7% of runtime, while simulated
rollouts are 96.2%, dominated by 362,164 Gaussian-process posterior evaluations
in a single truncated run. Implemented speed-ups validated by bit-identity
against stored traces, so any change that altered the optimizer's trajectory
would be rejected rather than mistaken for a speed improvement: replaced SciPy's
generic normal-CDF wrapper with the underlying error-function call (verified
bitwise identical on 200,000 random inputs, maximum difference exactly zero) and
eliminated a duplicate posterior construction computed twice per acquisition
call on identical inputs. Verified against a 50-digit arbitrary-precision
reference that the explicit Gaussian-density formula and SciPy's log-space route
are equally accurate (9.5e-16, 1.8e-15 and 2.8e-14 relative error in the
typical, tail and extreme regimes -- a tie in every regime), establishing that
substituting it changes reproducibility but not correctness.

================================================================================
5. EXPERIMENTAL RIGOUR (transferable methodology)
================================================================================

Ran the entire investigation under pre-registration: every hypothesis was
committed to version control with its predictions, thresholds and falsification
conditions before the corresponding data existed, making the git history a
timestamped record that plans preceded results. Built a verification gate
requiring bit-identical trajectories -- identical fidelity, location and value at
every single query -- before any shared-code change could be used, and validated
one instrumentation patch across two benchmarks and six seeds under it.
Developed tooling that catches classes of analysis error mechanically rather
than by vigilance: a checker that verifies a registered pass condition and its
falsifier actually partition the space of outcomes (so no result can land in an
unregistered gap and be interpreted after the fact), a cost-axis checker that
detects when two methods record their spending on different scales (a defect
that would have silently granted one method 25% more budget in a head-to-head
comparison), and a publication checker that blocks release of any claim already
retracted elsewhere in the record. Adopted the practice of naming, in each
protocol, the existing claim a result could force a retraction of -- which is
what caused several of this project's own conclusions to be withdrawn on
evidence rather than defended.

6. SUGGESTED FRAMINGS
================================================================================

Short (one line):
Extended a single-fidelity Bayesian optimization method to multi-fidelity,
integrating a cost-aware information-theoretic acquisition function and a
calibrated region-of-interest heuristic that together improve simple regret by
5.96% of optimum on 10 of 10 seeds.

Medium (two to three lines):
Built the multi-fidelity extension of a transformer-based Bayesian optimization
method, including a Kennedy-O'Hagan correlated surrogate, a vectorized joint
Max-value Entropy Search acquisition over both query location and fidelity, and
a region-of-interest heuristic calibrated by bisection to a target acceptance
rate (0.0999 realized against a 0.10 target, standard deviation 0.0001, across
four benchmarks). The resulting method places second of five on three of four
benchmarks, and the combined improvements reduce simple regret by 5.96% of
optimum on all 10 seeds while raising query quality by an effect size of 3.49.

   that catches analysis errors mechanically.

================================================================================
HONEST SCOPE (include or omit, but do not contradict)
================================================================================

The region-of-interest improvements are demonstrated on Borehole 8D and do not
transfer to the other three benchmarks tested. The method does not overtake the
strongest baseline (MF-MES) on Borehole: it closes roughly 6 of a 10-point gap
and remains ahead on 2 of 10 seeds. The method is also substantially slower in
wall-clock terms than that baseline, by a factor of 16 to 29, though the
evaluation-cost budget that the comparison holds fixed is the appropriate
currency for a sample-efficiency claim. State these as scope, not as failure:
they are the boundaries of a measured result rather than gaps in it.
