The RTG confound was a starved reward

MF-DRO · Hartmann 6D · autoresearch progress report · 2026-08-24

One-paragraph summary

Three separate experiments tried to make the Decision Transformer's return-to-go signal vary, and all three voided on their own manipulation checks. They share a single cause: under the current reward, a low-fidelity query earns exactly zero, so 63% of training trajectories carry a return of zero. A signal that is identically zero cannot be made to vary by rescaling it. The long-open question “does the network ignore RTG, or is RTG starved?” resolves to starved — with a fixable cause that was already implemented in the codebase.

The strongest result: it isn't the conditioning signal at all

H19 — the one fix the theory predicted

RCSL theory (Brandfonbrener et al., NeurIPS 2022) bounds suboptimality by ε(1/αf+3)H², where ε is departure from deterministic dynamics. Our rollout transition is a draw from the GP posterior, so ε is maximal. Their Figure 1c shows the bias then survives regardless of the conditioning function — which is what ten interventions found.

So we removed the stochasticity. fantasy_mode="mean" makes the transition deterministic (verified: repeat-difference 0.000e+00 vs 1.6e+00) with diversity intact (200/200 distinct trajectories).

The argmax still moved 0/12. In the exact regime the theory says should work.

So near-determinism is not the binding constraint. The cause is architectural, and H5 already measured it: swapping h for another state's hidden vector leaves the argmax unchanged 12/12. Nothing that reaches h can move a decision that is not a function of h.

Two claims of mine, retracted the same day

  • H18's “ε and αf are coupled” — withdrawn. It rested on diversity collapsing 131→62, but my signature was (rtg, ell) and omitted the query locations, so rollouts with a dead reward (63% of them) and a matching fidelity pattern counted as identical. With locations included every arm is 200/200. No collapse.
  • “RCSL theory predicts everything” — too strong. It explains why the conditioning-side remedies failed; it does not explain the architecture. H19 is the check that caught the overreach.

The pre-registered threshold (>150 distinct) was never moved. Only the measurement was corrected.

Two facts that needed no compute

1. The RTG band was capped by algebra, not by the network

target = max(batch_max, α·running_max),   batch_max ≤ running_max  ⇒  band ≤ 1/α

At α=0.5 the band cannot exceed 2×, whatever the reward or normalisation. Two experiments (H9, H10) burned ~30 minutes of compute against a ceiling provable in two lines; H10 measured 1.76× and 2.59×, both at it.

2. Joint MES was already implemented

The mes_entropy mode's return is log(b_τ) − log(b_T), where b_T is drawn from the fully-conditioned end-of-rollout model. Since H(Gumbel) = ln b + γ + 1:

log(b0) − log(bT) = H[y*|D0] − H[y*|DT] = I(y*; y1..T)

That is the joint, set-level information gain of the final dataset — not a per-step cumulative sum. LF credit is automatic: an LF observation shrinks the y* distribution through ρ, so its discount is derived from the fitted KO model rather than hand-specified.

Why it had been abandoned

The gate that dropped mes_entropy was within-group Spearman(rtg[0], true f_hf(x₀)) — where x₀ is the trajectory's first query.

That measures step-0 greediness. improvement is built from HF improvements, so it scores well by construction; an information-gain reward is designed to rate an exploratory first query highly when it shrinks H[y*]. The gate penalised exactly what the reward exists to do. H15 re-runs it against terminal trajectory quality, with a reproduction check on the original axis first.

Standing result (unchanged, and still the honest headline)

Frozen success test: FAIL. MF-DRO 0.5047 ± 0.0395 vs MF-MI-Greedy 0.5091 ± 0.1266 and MF-GP-UCB 1.7934 ± 0.1223 at matched cost ~200. The freeze pathology is resolved (0/10 frozen vs 9/12 pre-fix, after the target-leakage fix), but the performance gap does not close. Paired: better on 4/10, median +0.0915, Wilcoxon p = 0.275 — underpowered, never “equivalent”.

What this phase cost, honestly

  • Three voided experiments against a cap provable in two lines of algebra.
  • Two experiments (H12, H13) reinventing — worse — a reward the codebase already had. Spec before building.
  • One measurement bug in my own gate (tested rtg[0] ≤ 0 against a criterion written rtg[0] == 0; those differ once the reward can go negative). Caught, fixed, re-run rather than reinterpreted.
  • One decomposition rule that saturated at 100% in both compared arms and so said nothing.

The pre-registered manipulation checks did their job every time — nothing uninterpretable was reported as a result. But a gate is a safety net, and algebra would have been cheaper.
