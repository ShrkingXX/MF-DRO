MF-DRO re-fits; it does not condition

MF-DRO · Hartmann 6D · autoresearch progress report · 2026-08-24

One-paragraph summary

Three separate experiments tried to make the Decision Transformer's return-to-go signal vary, and all three voided on their own manipulation checks. They share a single cause: under the current reward, a low-fidelity query earns exactly zero, so 63% of training trajectories carry a return of zero. A signal that is identically zero cannot be made to vary by rescaling it. The long-open question “does the network ignore RTG, or is RTG starved?” resolves to starved — with a fixable cause that was already implemented in the codebase.

Two stages, two different causes (H21)

The encoder's contraction is architectural — a random transformer contracts almost as hard as a trained one (ratio 1.18). The head's is learned: a random head amplifies variation where the trained head contracts it, a 4.8× swing from fitting alone. Training actively collapses the scoring head.

But neither is near the threshold that would matter. Even with 5.7× more coefficient variation, the random network still moves the argmax 0/12 at cosine 0.99978. The invariance is far from tipping, not marginally short.

Guard fixed in advance and it binds: this is a channel-capacity probe only. An untrained network's choices are meaningless for performance, and nothing here suggests skipping training.

The student picks the teacher's top candidate on 5 of 12 pools and is never worse than 12th of 200. Corrected: I had called the distillation "lossy" from an 8/12 argmax agreement — a bad metric when the leading candidates are near-ties.

The fidelity head is a different story: p moves by 0.0007 across 24 decisions while the teacher's choice genuinely varies. ell ~ Bernoulli(p) is close to an unconditional coin flip.

So the transformer is redundant on the decision it approximates well, and uninformative on the one it does not.

Sliding-window inference — the user's test

Feeding the last 7 real queries plus the current one leaves every proposal bit-identical (max |Δx| = 0), same fidelity choices, same regret. Gate failed, so the 2-hour frozen run was not spent.

And it is not a no-op inside the network: the same real history moves the hidden state to cosine 0.895 and the coefficient vector by 11.2% — about 85× the 0.13% that ordinary state variation produces — and still reorders nothing. The shift lies in directions that do not change the candidate ranking.

The teacher, not the student (H28–H30)

Three corrections in three ticks — each narrowed the claim

H28. The MF-MES teacher's own choices sit at the 2.9th percentile of σH within their candidate pools (control: 94.2nd percentile of μH). The student's uncertainty aversion is inherited. Retracted: my claim that "the student inverted the sign of its teacher's defining term" — it inverts nothing.

H29. Intrinsic, not tuning. Sweeping cost ratio {2,4,8,16} × y* samples {5,10,50}, the chosen-σ percentile never exceeds 5.5% in any of 12 cells. Ten times more Monte Carlo changes nothing; an 8× cost swing moves it ~1%→~5%.

H30. And the negative coefficient is not an aversion. corr(μH, σH) = −0.4696 — in a GP the high-mean region is where data already sits. Under that collinearity the teacher's own score has a negative partial σ coefficient (−0.0419, negative on 91.4% of sets) despite a positive marginal correlation (+0.1585). Textbook suppression. Corrected: the paper's claim that the rule "penalises exactly what UCB/EI/MES reward" — MES's own partial coefficient is negative too.

What survives is behavioural, not coefficient-based: selections land in the bottom 3% of posterior uncertainty, in every regime tested. That does not depend on interpreting any weight.

Running now — the sharpest remaining test

H31: run the MF-MES teacher under the frozen evaluation with no Decision Transformer deciding at all. The DT is still trained (so the RNG stream matches and the teacher sees the identical candidate pools), but its output is discarded.

H24 found the student agrees with its teacher on only 8/12 pools, so the distillation is lossy. If the teacher alone does better, the transformer is a net negative — the sharpest possible form of this result. Reported either way.

And none of it is significant. Paired vs MI-Greedy: −0.1085, 6/10 seeds, Wilcoxon p = 0.432. The frozen success test FAILS (0.4481 ≥ 0.3825) — as it did for the first reward (0.5442). The gap narrowed by roughly half across two rewards. It did not close.

A full run's worth of state change (mean pairwise L2 = 1.4968) rotates the coefficient vector by 2.04° and changes the decision on 0 of 12 candidate pools — both across ensemble members and across real iterations. The fidelity head is equally inert (p spans 0.1248–0.1286 with weights fixed).

There is no single broken layer — the loss compounds, ~3× at the encoder and ~3× at the head. That is why every module-specific intervention could address at most half of it.

H17 is the only intervention that moved regret, and it changed the training signal, not the conditioning pathway. MF-DRO improves the way a re-fitted acquisition function improves, not the way a conditioned policy improves.

The strongest result: it isn't the conditioning signal at all

H19 — the one fix the theory predicted

RCSL theory (Brandfonbrener et al., NeurIPS 2022) bounds suboptimality by ε(1/αf+3)H², where ε is departure from deterministic dynamics. Our rollout transition is a draw from the GP posterior, so ε is maximal. Their Figure 1c shows the bias then survives regardless of the conditioning function — which is what ten interventions found.

So we removed the stochasticity. fantasy_mode="mean" makes the transition deterministic (verified: repeat-difference 0.000e+00 vs 1.6e+00) with diversity intact (200/200 distinct trajectories).

The argmax still moved 0/12. In the exact regime the theory says should work.

So near-determinism is not the binding constraint. The cause is architectural, and H5 already measured it: swapping h for another state's hidden vector leaves the argmax unchanged 12/12. Nothing that reaches h can move a decision that is not a function of h.

Two claims of mine, retracted the same day

  • H18's “ε and αf are coupled” — withdrawn. It rested on diversity collapsing 131→62, but my signature was (rtg, ell) and omitted the query locations, so rollouts with a dead reward (63% of them) and a matching fidelity pattern counted as identical. With locations included every arm is 200/200. No collapse.
  • “RCSL theory predicts everything” — too strong. It explains why the conditioning-side remedies failed; it does not explain the architecture. H19 is the check that caught the overreach.

Two facts that needed no compute

1. The RTG band was capped by algebra, not by the network

target = max(batch_max, α·running_max),   batch_max ≤ running_max  ⇒  band ≤ 1/α

At α=0.5 the band cannot exceed 2×, whatever the reward or normalisation. Two experiments (H9, H10) burned ~30 minutes of compute against a ceiling provable in two lines; H10 measured 1.76× and 2.59×, both at it.

2. Joint MES was already implemented

The mes_entropy mode's return is log(b_τ) − log(b_T), where b_T is drawn from the fully-conditioned end-of-rollout model. Since H(Gumbel) = ln b + γ + 1:

log(b0) − log(bT) = H[y*|D0] − H[y*|DT] = I(y*; y1..T)

That is the joint, set-level information gain of the final dataset — not a per-step cumulative sum. LF credit is automatic: an LF observation shrinks the y* distribution through ρ, so its discount is derived from the fitted KO model rather than hand-specified.

Why it had been abandoned

The gate that dropped mes_entropy was within-group Spearman(rtg[0], true f_hf(x₀)) — where x₀ is the trajectory's first query.

That measures step-0 greediness. improvement is built from HF improvements, so it scores well by construction; an information-gain reward is designed to rate an exploratory first query highly when it shrinks H[y*]. The gate penalised exactly what the reward exists to do. H15 re-runs it against terminal trajectory quality, with a reproduction check on the original axis first.

Standing result (unchanged, and still the honest headline)

Frozen success test: FAIL. MF-DRO 0.5047 ± 0.0395 vs MF-MI-Greedy 0.5091 ± 0.1266 and MF-GP-UCB 1.7934 ± 0.1223 at matched cost ~200. The freeze pathology is resolved (0/10 frozen vs 9/12 pre-fix, after the target-leakage fix), but the performance gap does not close. Paired: better on 4/10, median +0.0915, Wilcoxon p = 0.275 — underpowered, never “equivalent”.

What this phase cost, honestly

  • Three voided experiments against a cap provable in two lines of algebra.
  • Two experiments (H12, H13) reinventing — worse — a reward the codebase already had. Spec before building.
  • One measurement bug in my own gate (tested rtg[0] ≤ 0 against a criterion written rtg[0] == 0; those differ once the reward can go negative). Caught, fixed, re-run rather than reinterpreted.
  • One decomposition rule that saturated at 100% in both compared arms and so said nothing.

The pre-registered manipulation checks did their job every time — nothing uninterpretable was reported as a result. But a gate is a safety net, and algebra would have been cheaper.
