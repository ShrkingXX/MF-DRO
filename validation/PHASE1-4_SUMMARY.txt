================================================================================
JOINT RTG VALIDATION: PHASE 1-4 SUMMARY
================================================================================
Investigation into whether the Decision Transformer's RTG training label
should switch from the current per-step reward-sum formulation to a "joint"
formulation: RTG[tau] = log(b_tau / b_T), the log-ratio of Gumbel scale
parameters (fit via Thompson sampling on the GP posterior) before and after
a simulated rollout. b parameterizes an entropy estimate of y* = max_x f(x),
so this log-ratio directly measures how much a rollout reduced uncertainty
about the global optimum.

Benchmarks referenced: Ackley_2D, Ackley_5D, Rosenbrock_2D, Hartmann_6D,
Currin_2D. Stages: early (5 real points), mid (25), late (45).

Full JSON results: validation/results/phase1_gumbel_quality.json,
phase1_thompson_gumbel.json, phase2_rtg_correlation.json (v3),
phase2_rtg_correlation_v4.json, phase3_scale_check.json (v3),
phase3_scale_check_v4.json. Scripts: validation/phase1_*.py, phase2_*.py,
phase3_*.py, phase4_*.py. All phases 1-3 are read-only diagnostics (do not
modify src/policy/dro.py); Phase 4 makes real production changes to dro.py.


================================================================================
PHASE 1: GUMBEL APPROXIMATION QUALITY
================================================================================
Question: is the Gumbel distribution a good fit to the true max-value
distribution of y* = max_x f(x), which the "joint" RTG's entropy term
requires?

--- Phase 1 original: product-CDF Gumbel approximation ---
Method: closed-form Gumbel parameters fit via 25th/75th-percentile matching
of the product-of-independent-CDFs formula (same method used elsewhere in
the codebase's MES reward, mes_reward.py). Compared against empirical
maxima drawn from the TRUE (correlated) GP posterior via joint rsample().

Result: 0/15 (benchmark, stage) combinations PASS, 4/15 WARN, 11/15 FAIL.
KS statistic range: 0.158 - 0.862 (threshold: PASS<0.15, WARN<=0.25).
Worst failures on Ackley_2D (KS 0.57-0.85) and Rosenbrock_2D (KS 0.73-0.86).

Diagnosis: the product-CDF formula assumes independence across candidate
points, which is violated under RBF-kernel posterior correlation. This is
the source of the failure, not the Gumbel family itself.

--- Phase 1 re-run: Thompson-sampling-based Gumbel fit ---
Method: same benchmarks/stages, but y* samples drawn via TRUE joint
rsample() from the GP posterior (Thompson sampling, K=500), then Gumbel(a,b)
fit via scipy MLE (gumbel_r.fit) to those samples directly -- no
independence assumption anywhere.

Result: 15/15 PASS. KS statistic range: 0.034 - 0.102.

CONCLUSION: the Gumbel family is a correct model for y*'s distribution.
The failure in Phase 1 original was entirely due to the independence
assumption in the product-CDF shortcut, not a problem with using Gumbel
per se. Thompson sampling + MLE fit is the right method going forward.


================================================================================
PHASE 2: PER-STEP RTG vs JOINT RTG CORRELATION
================================================================================
Question: on matched simulated rollouts, how well does joint RTG[0]
(= log(b_0/b_T), via Thompson+Gumbel) correlate with the current per-step
RTG[0] (backward cumsum of rewards)? High correlation would mean the two
formulations are close to interchangeable as DT training targets.

Method: validation/phase2_rtg_correlation.py's simulate_with_gumbel_b
reimplements the rollout loop with GP fantasy-conditioning
(condition_on_observations) at each step (since _simulate_trajectory itself
does NOT fantasy-condition -- confirmed by direct code reading), and a
FIXED roi_candidates set computed once before the rollout loop (not
regenerated each step, per explicit instruction to avoid resampling noise).
100 rollouts x 4-step trajectories per (benchmark, stage). Also checks an
entropy-monotonicity diagnostic (H_0 >= H_T should hold; more conditioning
= less uncertainty about y*).

--- CRITICAL BUG FOUND (before v4) ---
validation/phase2_rtg_correlation.py:120 had:
    roi_candidates, _ = optimizer._optimize_acquisition(gp_idx, observed_best)
_optimize_acquisition returns (best_x, roi_candidates) in that order (every
other call site in the codebase gets this right, including
_simulate_trajectory and both Phase 1 scripts). This line silently swapped
them: "roi_candidates" was actually the single best_x point (shape [1,D]),
discarding the real ~1000-point domain-spanning candidate set. Consequences:
  1. x_tau selection always picked the same single point at every rollout
     step (rollouts never actually explored the domain).
  2. Thompson sampling degenerated to the marginal predictive distribution
     at that one point, not a genuine max-over-domain estimate -- so b_tau
     was measuring "variance at one repeatedly-conditioned point" rather
     than "uncertainty about the global max."
  3. The MES reward's Term 2 quadrature also degenerated to a single-
     candidate integral.
Fixed by swapping the assignment order. Phase 3 reuses this same function
(simulate_with_gumbel_b), so it inherited the identical bug.

PHASE 3: RTG SCALE COMPATIBILITY (Hartmann_6D, Currin_2D only)
================================================================================
Question: is joint RTG's scale compatible with the existing floored-dynamic
RTG schema (target = max(batch_max, alpha_floor * running_max)), and is the
signal strong enough (relative to its own noise) to be a useful DT target?
Scoped to Hartmann_6D/Currin_2D per Phase 2's finding that the other 3
benchmarks show ~41-58% negative-RTG fraction regardless.

Method: regenerates 100 rollouts x 3 stages via the same
simulate_with_gumbel_b (so it also inherited, and was fixed by, the same
roi_candidates bug as Phase 2). Reports scale_ratio (per-step-RTG-mean /
joint-RTG-mean), SNR (joint-RTG mean/std), neg_frac.
PASS: 0.2<=scale_ratio<=5.0, SNR>=0.5, neg_frac<0.15.

================================================================================
PHASE 4: PRODUCTION IMPLEMENTATION AND EMPIRICAL VALIDATION (Currin_2D only)
================================================================================

--- Step 1: implementation ---
rtg_schema="joint" implemented as a real option in
DirectRegretOptimization (src/policy/dro.py):
  - New method _simulate_trajectory_joint: mirrors the validated (bugfixed)
    simulate_with_gumbel_b -- roi_candidates computed once per rollout, GP
    fantasy-conditioned via condition_on_observations at each step,
    Thompson-sampled b_tau/b_T, joint_rtg[tau]=log(b_tau/b_T) stored
    unclamped.
  - _propose_next_candidate dispatches to _simulate_trajectory_joint and
    uses trajectory['joint_rtg'][0] (not rewards.sum()) when
    rtg_schema=="joint".
  - _compute_rtg_target's "floored" branch extended to also cover "joint"
    (same formula; Phase 3 confirmed dimensional compatibility).
  - _train_decision_transformer reads traj['joint_rtg'][:traj_len] directly
    as the RTG label for this schema, bypassing the backward-cumsum-of-
    rewards computation used by all other schemas.
  - New shared module gumbel_thompson.py created (thompson_sample_y_star,
    fit_gumbel_to_samples) to avoid a circular import: dro.py importing
    directly from validation/phase1_thompson_gumbel.py would cycle back
    through phase1_gumbel_quality.py's own "from src.policy.dro import
    DirectRegretOptimization".
  - rtg_schema validation whitelist in __init__ extended to include "joint".

--- Step 2: debug checks (Currin_2D, seed=42, 5 rollouts) ---
7 checks on the real production class (not a re-implementation), all core
checks PASS: roi_candidates sanity (real domain-spanning set, not a single
point), fantasy-conditioning has a measurable effect across rollouts,
joint_rtg has no NaN/Inf, _propose_next_candidate dispatch confirmed,
_compute_rtg_target merged floored/joint formula correct,
_train_decision_transformer trains on joint_rtg (not reward-cumsum),
confirmed both statically (source inspection) and dynamically (corrupted-
rewards test).

Follow-up: neg_frac at seed=42, early (n_initial=5) vs mid (n_initial=25)
stage, N=30 rollouts each:
  early: neg_frac=40.0%, joint_rtg[0] mean=0.0104 std=0.0923,
         b_0=0.5684 b_T=0.5624 (barely any entropy decrease -- noise-level)
  mid:   neg_frac=3.3%,  joint_rtg[0] mean=0.9638 std=0.1540,
         b_0=0.3687 b_T=0.1403 (b_T is ~38% of b_0 -- strong, real signal)
Interpretation (per pre-declared decision rule): problem confined to the
first handful of iterations; proceed to Step 3.

--- Step 3: full empirical comparison ---
DRO-MES-PerStep vs DRO-MES-Joint, Currin_2D, seeds 42/43/44, 30 BO
iterations, 75 rollouts/iteration, use_mes_reward=True for both.

Final simple regret (mean +/- SE, N=3 seeds):
  DRO-MES-PerStep:  0.1997 +/- 0.1170
  DRO-MES-Joint:    1.5534 +/- 0.6330   (~8x worse)

Per-seed final regret:
  seed   PerStep    Joint
  42     0.0048     2.4833  (Joint: FLAT for all 30 iterations, 0 improvements)
  43     0.1151     0.0139  (Joint: 1 improvement at iter ~2-3, then flat for 28 iters)
  44     0.4792     2.1629  (Joint: FLAT for all 30 iterations, 0 improvements)

DRO-MES-PerStep shows continuous multi-step improvement on every seed.
DRO-MES-Joint: 3/3 seeds show 0-1 total improving iterations out of 30.

--- Root-cause diagnosis ---
Reproduced one real iteration's 75-rollout batch (iteration 1, seed 42) for
both variants using the exact production rollout-generation logic:
                    mean     std     min      max    CV(std/mean)
DRO-MES-PerStep    0.0412   0.0169  0.0170   0.0826    0.41
DRO-MES-Joint      0.0260   0.1301 -0.3177   0.2999    5.01

PerStep's batch is tight, always-positive, low relative noise. Joint's
batch is centered near ZERO with std 5x the mean -- roughly half the batch
is negative, meaning the metric is dominated by Thompson-sampling/Gumbel-
MLE noise at this stage, not real signal (consistent with the Step 2
early-stage neg_frac/b_0-vs-b_T finding above).

Mechanism: _compute_rtg_target uses
    rtg_target = max(batch_max_rtg, alpha_floor * running_max_rtg)
and running_max_rtg is a RATCHET that only ever increases, never decays
(confirmed in __init__: self.running_max_rtg = 0.0, only ever updated via
max()). batch_max_rtg = max() of 75 rollouts mostly selects a rare noise
spike for Joint (not a real achievable value); because running_max_rtg
never decays, that early noise spike permanently inflates the floor for
every later iteration -- even once the GP has 20-30 real points and the
underlying joint-RTG signal genuinely improves (per Phase 2's own mid/late-
stage numbers). Confirmed empirically: seed 42's rtg_target climbed from
0.30 (iter 1) to 1.90 (iter 30); seed 44's running_max_rtg climbed to 2.99.
The DT is left extrapolating to an increasingly out-of-distribution target
it never learned a reliable action mapping for, producing the observed
degenerate/stuck policy.

--- Attempted fix: DRO-MES-Joint-P90 ---
Change: batch_max_rtg = 90th percentile of batch_rtg0_list (instead of raw
max), scoped to rtg_schema=="joint" only (src/policy/dro.py
_compute_rtg_target). Same 3-seed, 30-iteration comparison re-run.

Final simple regret (mean +/- SE, N=3 seeds):
  DRO-MES-Joint-P90: 1.5467 +/- 0.6358   (statistically unchanged vs 1.5534)

Per-seed final regret:
  seed 42: 2.4772  (was fully flat; now exactly 1 tiny improvement at iter 20,
                     0.006 change -- still essentially stuck)
  seed 43: 0.00007 (was 1 early improvement then flat; still 1 early
                     improvement then flat, lands marginally better)
  seed 44: 2.1629  (still completely flat, 0/30 improvements, unchanged)

CONCLUSION: percentile alone reduces target noise (smoother, less
extreme early rtg_target values) but does NOT resolve the lock-in -- 2/3
seeds remain almost entirely stuck. The non-decaying running_max_rtg
ratchet is at least as important as the noisy max(); a real fix would
likely need BOTH a more robust batch statistic AND a decaying or warmup-
gated running_max_rtg. Out of scope for this investigation.


================================================================================
FINAL RECOMMENDATION
================================================================================
KEEP PER-STEP RTG (rtg_schema="floored") IN PRODUCTION.

Joint RTG is theoretically well-motivated (Phase 1: the Gumbel family is a
correct model for y*, once Thompson sampling replaces the flawed product-
CDF approximation) and passes WARN-level correlation/scale checks in
isolated validation on Currin_2D specifically (Phases 2-3). But the
empirical 3-seed comparison (Phase 4 Step 3) shows it produces a
substantially worse and often-degenerate policy in practice on that exact
benchmark: the theoretical gap between the formulations matters a great
deal empirically, not just marginally, and a straightforward percentile-
based fix to the most obvious failure mode (noisy batch-max selection) is
insufficient on its own.

A viable joint RTG implementation would require redesigning the target-
selection mechanism -- a more robust batch statistic AND a decaying or
warmup-gated running-max ratchet -- rather than reusing the existing
floored-schema machinery unchanged. This is a well-defined but currently
unexplored direction for future work.

Code state: rtg_schema="joint" (and the "joint"-only percentile variant
inside _compute_rtg_target) remain in src/policy/dro.py as real, working
options -- not deleted, just not the recommended default. All validation
scripts, plots, and this summary are preserved under validation/ for
reproducibility.

Plots: validation/results/plots/ (Phase 3 v3), plots_v4/ (Phase 3 v4),
plots_phase4/ (Phase 4 Step 4: regret curves, batch distribution,
running_max_rtg trajectories, final regret summary -- also copied into
results/ and referenced from results/MF_DRO_results.tex and report.tex,
section "Phase 4: Joint RTG Empirical Validation").
